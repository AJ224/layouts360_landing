## What is site visit management software?

**Site visit management software** schedules a buyer's visit to your project, logs exactly what happened during the visit—which plots were shown, what the buyer asked, what objections came up—and triggers the right follow-up automatically. It connects the visit to the buyer's CRM record and to the real plots on your layout, not just a line in a diary.

Most teams already schedule visits informally. What is usually missing is the record of what happened afterward, and a way to measure whether visits are actually converting to bookings.

**In one sentence:** site visit management software turns a calendar appointment into a trackable step in the sales pipeline, linked to real plots and real follow-up.

## Who is this for?

### Developers and builders running active site visits weekly

If your team hosts multiple visits a week, you need visibility into who visited, what they were shown, and what happened next—without relying on agents' memory or personal notebooks.

### Sales managers coaching field agents

You want to see visit-to-booking conversion by agent to identify who needs help with follow-up discipline versus who needs help getting more visits scheduled.

### Teams selling to NRI and remote buyers

Virtual site visits over video call, walking buyers through the live digital layout map, need the same tracking and follow-up rigor as in-person visits.

### Channel partners bringing buyers to site

Partners who arrange their own buyer visits need those visits logged and attributed correctly for lead ownership and commission purposes.

## Problems teams face without site visit tracking

### Visits happen, then disappear into agent memory

A buyer visits, likes two plots, and asks for a follow-up call in three days—but that detail lives only in the agent's head or a personal notebook, not a system anyone else can see.

### No visibility into conversion by agent or source

Leadership cannot tell whether a low booking rate is due to too few visits, poor visit quality, or weak follow-up—because visit outcomes are not tracked systematically.

### Blocked plots from visits are never released

A plot blocked "just in case" during a visit stays blocked indefinitely because no one remembers to release it after the buyer decides against it.

### Partner-sourced visits create ownership disputes

When a partner brings a buyer for a site visit without a clear record, disputes arise later over who gets credit and commission if the buyer eventually books.

## How Layouts360 solves it

Layouts360 treats a site visit as a structured, trackable step in the sales pipeline. Concretely:

- Visits scheduled directly from a CRM lead, with planned plots and assigned agent recorded
- Post-visit logging captures plots shown, buyer feedback, and agreed next step
- Automatic reminders to reduce no-shows for both buyer and agent
- Plot holds tied to a visit expire automatically if the buyer does not proceed

## How it works

1. **Schedule the visit** — From a CRM lead, set the date, assigned agent, and plots of interest.
2. **Remind automatically** — Both buyer and agent receive a reminder before the scheduled time.
3. **Conduct the visit** — Agent shows the relevant plots, using the digital layout map on-site if helpful.
4. **Log the outcome** — Plots actually shown, buyer feedback, objections, and next step are recorded on mobile.
5. **Follow up on schedule** — The agreed next step becomes a task tied to the same CRM record.
6. **Track conversion** — Reports show visit-to-booking rate by agent, project, or lead source.

## Rollout playbook for site visit management

**Week 1:** Require every scheduled visit to be logged in the CRM with assigned agent and plots of interest before it happens. Turn on automatic reminders for buyers and agents.

**Week 2:** Make post-visit logging mandatory within a set window (for example, same day) and review a sample of logs for quality and completeness.

**Month 1:** Review visit-to-booking conversion by agent and source. Identify agents who generate visits but do not convert, versus those who convert well but need more leads.

**Red flags:** Visits happen without being scheduled in the system; post-visit logs are consistently blank or generic; blocked plots from old visits are never released.

## Making site visit follow-up a habit, not a hope

Most sales teams already know that fast, specific follow-up after a site visit improves conversion. The gap is rarely intent—it is that the details needed for a good follow-up call (which plots, what concern, what was promised) are not written down anywhere the next shift or the agent's own memory a week later can retrieve reliably.

A simple discipline closes most of this gap: no site visit is considered complete until the log is filled in, and no lead moves forward in the pipeline without a next step and a date attached. Managers who enforce just these two rules typically see follow-up consistency improve faster than from any automation feature alone—the system supports the habit, but the habit has to be set first.

### Measuring what a "good" visit-to-booking rate looks like

Visit-to-booking conversion varies widely by project stage, price point, and city, so there is no single universal benchmark to chase. What matters more is tracking your own project's rate over time and by agent, so a declining trend or a consistent gap between two agents selling the same inventory becomes visible early—before it shows up as a missed sales target at quarter-end.
