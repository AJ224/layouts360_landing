## What is township management software?

**Township management software** is a system for tracking inventory, sales, customers, and channel partners across an entire multi-phase plotted development—not just the current launch. A township project usually spans several years and multiple phases, each with its own release date, pricing, and mix of plots, villas, or commercial plots.

Generic project management tools track tasks and timelines. Township management software, as Layouts360 builds it, tracks **sellable inventory**: which plot in which phase is available, blocked, booked, or sold, who owns the lead, and how much has been collected against that unit.

**In one sentence:** township management software gives developers one live map and one database for every phase of a plotted township, so sales, finance, and partners never work from conflicting versions of availability.

## Who is this for?

### Township developers and master-plan promoters

You are selling a project designed to unfold over years, not weeks. You need phase-level control with a project-wide view for board reporting, lender updates, and pricing decisions on the next release.

### Builders running mixed plot and villa inventory

Many townships combine open plots, villa plots, and commercial corners in one master layout. Your system needs to handle different attributes and pricing logic for each inventory type without running three separate spreadsheets.

### Sales teams managing several active phases at once

Field agents often sell more than one phase simultaneously to different buyer segments. They need one login and one map that clearly separates phases while keeping plot lookup fast on site.

### Channel partner networks growing with each phase

Brokers who sold Phase 1 well should be trusted with Phase 3 and Phase 4 too—without your office manually re-sharing spreadsheets or PDF maps every quarter.

## Problems township developers face

### Phase confusion

Teams accidentally quote Phase 2 pricing on a Phase 4 plot, or a broker pitches a plot that was released two phases ago and already sold.

### Fragmented reporting

Leadership asks "how much of the township is sold overall?" and the answer requires manually combining five different phase-wise spreadsheets, each maintained by a different team member.

### Amenity-based pricing drift

Premium plots near the clubhouse or main road get priced inconsistently across phases because pricing logic lives in someone's head, not in a shared system.

### Partner access that does not scale

A partner network built for Phase 1 launch weekend does not automatically extend cleanly into Phase 5—broker onboarding restarts from scratch, or worse, old brokers keep sharing outdated PDFs from years ago.

### Long sales cycles losing institutional memory

Staff turnover over a multi-year township means new hires inherit incomplete records, and buyer history from earlier phases gets lost when the person who "knew everything" leaves.

## How it works

1. **Set up the township project** — Create the master project, then add each phase as it is planned or released.
2. **Digitize each phase** — Import plot and villa numbers, dimensions, facing, and phase-specific pricing.
3. **Publish live phase maps** — Sales and partners see current-phase availability alongside a project-wide overview.
4. **Sell and track** — Leads, site visits, blocks, and bookings tie to specific plots within the correct phase.
5. **Collect and reconcile** — Payments and installments are tracked per booking, rolling up into phase and project totals.
6. **Review and release the next phase** — Leadership uses sell-through data from the current phase to time and price the next one.

## Rollout playbook for a new township phase

**Week 1:** Digitize the new phase's plot and villa master with numbers, dimensions, and base pricing. Confirm the phase links correctly to the overall township project for roll-up reporting.

**Week 2:** Onboard or extend channel partner access to the new phase. Brief the sales team on any pricing or amenity differences versus the previous phase.

**Month 1:** Compare early sell-through of the new phase against previous phases at the same point in their cycle. Use this to calibrate pricing for the phase after next.

**Red flags:** Sales team still tracks a shadow spreadsheet "just for this phase"; partners are re-onboarded from scratch instead of extended; phase-level pricing decisions are not documented in the system.
