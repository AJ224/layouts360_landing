## Launch day: one map, many desks

**Launch day plot sales software** exists for the hour when paper charts fail. Hundreds of buyers, dozens of brokers, and multiple desks must read one availability truth with timed blocks and clear approvals.

Run a rehearsal Thursday: block, expire, convert to booking, and show partner view updating. Launch morning is too late to discover roles were never configured.

Leadership war-room metrics are blocks expiring, bookings confirmed, and partner queue depth—not verbal updates from the loudest desk. Debrief with booking IDs; that log trains the next phase better than anecdotal stories alone.


Launch maturity is measured in booking logs, not war stories. Teams that debrief with IDs improve hold policy before the next phase. Brokers return when launches are fair—timed blocks, visible queues, consistent approvals. Developers who repeat paper-chart launches lose partner goodwill even if they sell through eventually.

## Rollout playbook for launch day

**Week 1:** Freeze scope—one map, timed blocks, defined approvers. Ban parallel availability spreadsheets for launch weekend.

**Week 2:** Rehearse with brokers: block, expire, book, and refund edge case. Leadership watches dashboard during rehearsal, not paper tallies.

**Launch day:** War room tracks blocks expiring, bookings confirmed, partner queue, and payment failures. Extend blocks only through system roles with logged reason.

**After launch:** Debrief with booking IDs; update hold policy before next phase. Waitlists and expired blocks release inventory fairly—document every override.

**Red flags:** Multiple desks updating paper charts; verbal holds without timer; partners not on portal during high-traffic event. Launch software fails when culture keeps shadow processes.



### Handoff to leadership

Launch retrospective uses booking IDs and timestamps—not anecdotes. Policy updates for hold length and approver roles happen before the next phase, not repeated verbally. War room staffing plan names who extends blocks and who settles payment failures live.

### Training rhythm

Thursday rehearsal mandatory for brokers above ten count. Roles printed: desk, partner queue, finance bridge, tech contact. Post-launch: thirty-minute debrief same day while memory is fresh; document three process fixes maximum to implement before marketing restarts.

## After the launch weekend

The best launch teams treat the event as a process, not a one-day heroics story. Within forty-eight hours, release expired blocks, reconcile partner queues, and document disputes with booking IDs. Policy updates for hold length and approver roles should ship before the next phase—not repeated verbally at the cabin. Brokers remember fairness; they return when the map was authoritative under pressure.
