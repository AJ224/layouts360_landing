**Commercial plot inventory software** helps developers sell shops, showrooms, and commercial plots with the same discipline as residential layouts—unit-level availability, pricing rules, and broker alignment.

## Commercial sales differences

### Per square foot pricing

Quotes depend on area, road frontage, and corner premiums—not only plot number.

### Fewer units, higher stakes

One wrong availability story is costly.

### Broker-heavy channels

Commercial often relies on established CP networks.

## What software should handle

- Unit IDs on digital layout or floor-style plan
- Live available / hold / sold
- CRM with commercial buyer profiles
- Booking and installment tracking
- Partner portal with same inventory

## Evaluation checklist

1. Per sq. ft. and premium rules
2. Multi-project roll-up
3. Mobile site-office demos
4. Partner live access
5. Payment milestones

## High-street vs plotted commercial

High-street shop sales may use floor-style plans; open commercial plots use land maps. Ensure your vendor supports how you actually draw inventory—not only residential layout shapes.

## Investor vs end-user buyers

Commercial investors ask for rental yield assumptions outside software scope, but they still need accurate unit availability and payment schedules. Keep financial projections in brochures; keep inventory truth in the platform.

## Reporting for leadership

Roll up sold shop count, collection %, and unsold corner units weekly. Commercial moves slower than mass plots—trends matter more than hour-one spikes.

## Demo questions for vendors

Ask to block one shop, record token, and show partner view updating. If that takes more than five minutes in demo, implementation will hurt.

## Getting started

Start with one commercial phase, import unit master, and run two weeks parallel to Excel before full cutover.
