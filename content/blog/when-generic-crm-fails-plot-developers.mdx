Many Indian layout teams “already have CRM.” They use **Zoho**, **HubSpot**, **Salesforce**, or a spreadsheet labeled CRM. Follow-ups improve for a while. Then launch weekend arrives—and two agents sell the same plot because **inventory never lived in the CRM**.

This article explains when **generic CRM fails plot developers**, what symptoms to watch, and when to move to **real estate CRM software** that understands plots.

## What generic CRM does well

Credit where it is due. Generic CRMs excel at:

- Capturing web and ad leads  
- Email sequences and task reminders  
- Basic pipeline reporting  
- Integrating marketing tools  

If you only sell relationships and inventory is trivial, they can be enough.

Plot and layout sales are not that world.

## What generic CRM does not know

Out of the box, HubSpot/Zoho/Salesforce do not understand:

- Plot 42 in Phase 2 on a digital layout  
- Timed holds that expire for every broker  
- Satellite maps buyers use on site  
- Partner portals tied to live availability  
- Installments on a specific unit booking  

Teams “solve” this with custom fields and a parallel Excel map. That hybrid is where failures hide.

## Seven symptoms you have outgrown generic CRM

### 1. Excel is still the availability master

CRM has contacts. Excel has truth. Agents trust Excel when they conflict—until two Excels disagree.

### 2. Agents promise sold plots

The CRM deal is open; the plot sold two hours ago. Nobody updated the sheet.

### 3. Site visits lack plot context

Managers ask “did you call?” instead of “which plots did you show?” Coaching stays shallow.

### 4. Brokers work outside the system

Partners get PDF maps on WhatsApp. Your CRM never sees their activity until a fight starts.

### 5. Launch days ignore CRM entirely

When demand spikes, the team reverts to paper charts and verbal holds. Software that is abandoned on the most important day is the wrong software.

### 6. Seat costs explode

You add trainees and brokers. Per-seat CRM pricing punishes growth while inventory remains unsolved.

### 7. Marketing celebrates leads, sales bleeds refunds

Lead volume rises. Double-booking and trust issues rise faster. CRM dashboards look healthy; the business does not.

If three or more symptoms are true, you do not need “better CRM hygiene.” You need **inventory-native CRM**.

## When you should keep generic CRM (for now)

Stay put temporarily if:

- You sell through a single agent with under ~30 units  
- You have zero broker network  
- You are not launching soon  
- You already committed annually and can bridge with strict Excel discipline for one phase only  

Even then, plan the migration before the next broker weekend. Excel discipline rarely survives scale.

## When you should switch now

Switch before the next launch if:

- Broker count is growing  
- You have had (or nearly had) a double booking  
- NRIs buy from shared links  
- Leadership cannot trust sold % without a war room  
- You are paying for CRM seats *and* still employing a full-time Excel reconciler  

Waiting until after a public dispute is the expensive option.

## How to migrate without chaos

**Week 1:** Digitize Phase 1 into plot management / inventory. Make the map the availability master. Keep generic CRM read-only for old contacts if needed.

**Week 2:** Require plot interest on every new lead in the new system. Train site staff on mobile block-and-book. Stop PDF forwards to brokers.

**Week 3:** Move active pipeline deals that reference open plots. Close or archive CRM deals tied to sold units.

**Month 1:** Turn off dual entry. If someone updates Excel “just in case,” retrain—do not add features.

## FAQ

### Can we integrate Zoho with a separate inventory tool?

Sometimes. Integrations still fail when agents skip updates under launch pressure. One database beats two syncing tools.

### Is Salesforce customizable enough?

At enough budget and time, almost anything is. Most mid-market layout developers need go-live in days, not a six-month customization project.

### Will we lose email automation?

Many teams keep light marketing tools and move sales ops to inventory-native CRM. Winning the plot matters more than another drip sequence.
